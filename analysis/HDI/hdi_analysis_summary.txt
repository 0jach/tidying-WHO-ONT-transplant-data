HDI vs Organ Donation & Transplantation — Analysis Summary
===========================================================

Datasets used:
- WHO Global Observatory on Donation and Transplantation (donors_tidy_total.csv, transplant_tidy.csv)
- UNDP Human Development Index (humandevelopmentindex.csv)
- 122 countries matched across datasets, covering 2000–2023

HDI tiers used throughout:
- Low: HDI < 0.55
- Medium: 0.55 – 0.70
- High: 0.70 – 0.80
- Very High: 0.80+


1. HDI is a strong predictor of organ donation capacity
-------------------------------------------------------

Correlation between HDI and deceased donors per million population:
- Pearson r = 0.62 (p < 0.001)
- Spearman rho = 0.75 (p < 0.001)

Correlation between HDI and transplants per million population:
- Pearson r = 0.68 (p < 0.001)
- Spearman rho = 0.77 (p < 0.001)

The gap between Pearson and Spearman suggests a non-linear relationship:
donation rates accelerate past a development threshold rather than
climbing in a straight line.

Median rates by HDI tier:
- Low:       ~0 donors PMP,   0.5 transplants PMP
- Medium:    ~0 donors PMP,   3.3 transplants PMP
- High:     1.7 donors PMP,   8.9 transplants PMP
- Very High: 13.5 donors PMP, 41.5 transplants PMP

A simple linear model yields:
donors_pmp = 51.3 * HDI - 32.2


2. Living vs. deceased donor shift
-----------------------------------

Low-HDI countries rely almost entirely on living donors (99.5%).
As development increases, deceased donation becomes dominant.

Donor source breakdown by HDI tier:
- Low:       0.5% deceased, 99.5% living
- Medium:   28.6% deceased, 71.4% living
- High:     68.4% deceased, 31.6% living
- Very High: 74.1% deceased, 25.7% living

This reflects the absence of brain-death legislation, intensive care
infrastructure, and organ procurement organizations in lower-HDI
countries. Deceased donation unlocks multi-organ retrieval from a
single donor, which is why transplant volumes scale so much faster
once that infrastructure is in place.


3. Organ diversity increases with development
----------------------------------------------

Low-HDI countries perform almost exclusively kidney transplants,
because kidneys can come from living donors with relatively simpler
surgical infrastructure. As HDI rises, more complex organ transplants
become feasible.

Organ transplant mix by HDI tier (%):
- Low:       kidney 92%, liver 8%, lung 0%, pancreas 0%
- Medium:    kidney 81%, liver 19%, lung 0.5%, pancreas 0.1%
- High:      kidney 75%, liver 22%, lung 1.2%, pancreas 0.7%
- Very High: kidney 66%, liver 26%, lung 3.7%, pancreas 2.2%

Lung and pancreas transplants require deceased donors and highly
specialized surgical teams, which is why they only appear at
meaningful levels in very high HDI countries.


4. Policy matters: overperformers and underperformers
------------------------------------------------------

A linear regression (donors PMP ~ HDI) reveals countries that
perform far above or below what their development level predicts.

Top overperformers (more donors than HDI predicts):
1. Spain         (+26.0 residual)
2. Portugal      (+16.0)
3. Croatia       (+13.1)
4. France        (+11.9)
5. Belgium       (+11.7)
6. Belarus        (+9.7)
7. Austria        (+9.2)
8. Czechia        (+9.2)
9. Italy          (+9.0)

Spain's opt-out legislation and dedicated transplant coordination
model (the "Spanish Model") is the most well-known example of
policy-driven success in organ donation.

Top underperformers (fewer donors than HDI predicts):
1. Japan         (-13.8 residual)
2. UAE           (-11.1)
3. Singapore     (-11.1)
4. Brunei         (-9.5)
5. Kazakhstan     (-9.3)
6. Barbados       (-9.2)
7. Qatar          (-9.1)
8. Oman           (-9.1)
9. Georgia        (-8.7)
10. Albania       (-8.4)

Japan and Singapore underperform despite very high HDI, likely due
to cultural attitudes toward brain death and bodily integrity that
limit deceased donation consent rates.
